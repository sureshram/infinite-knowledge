# Infinite Knowledge — Product Strategy

**Vision:** Teach every person on the planet about everything in the universe.

---

## Stories, Topics & Features

1. **Story 1 — Science: Understand the Laws That Govern the Universe**
   *As a curious learner, I want to explore the fundamental principles of science so that I can understand how the physical world — from subatomic particles to galaxies — actually works.*

   1. **Topic 1 — Physics: Forces, Energy & Motion**
      1. Interactive simulations of Newton's laws with real-world scenarios (cars braking, rockets launching)
      2. Visual explainer of the electromagnetic spectrum with everyday examples (radio waves to gamma rays)
      3. Step-by-step equation builder that scaffolds learners from arithmetic to F = ma
      4. "What if?" sandbox where learners alter gravity or friction and observe outcomes
      5. Milestone quiz that unlocks a short documentary on Einstein's Theory of Relativity

   2. **Topic 2 — Biology: Life, Cells & Evolution**
      1. 3D cell explorer with zoom-and-tap annotations for organelles and their functions
      2. Animated DNA replication walkthrough with pause-and-explain moments
      3. Evolutionary timeline slider showing species divergence over 500 million years
      4. Ecosystem simulation where learners remove or add species and observe cascade effects
      5. Personalized flashcard deck auto-generated from taxonomy and classification lessons

   3. **Topic 3 — Chemistry: Matter, Reactions & the Periodic Table**
      1. Interactive periodic table with element stories, discovery history, and real-world uses
      2. Virtual lab for balancing chemical equations with drag-and-drop molecule building
      3. Reaction visualizer showing bond breaking and forming at the molecular level
      4. Safety-first scenario cards explaining why certain household chemicals must never be mixed
      5. Progress tracker mapping mastery across element groups, reaction types, and states of matter

   4. **Topic 4 — Astronomy: Stars, Planets & the Cosmos**
      1. Real-time sky map using device GPS that identifies visible stars and constellations
      2. Scale model of the solar system learners can scroll through to internalize vast distances
      3. Life-cycle of a star animation from nebula through main sequence to supernova or white dwarf
      4. "Mission Control" challenges where learners plot orbital trajectories to reach a destination planet
      5. Daily astronomy news feed curated at reading level, tied to current lesson topics

   5. **Topic 5 — Earth Science: Geology, Climate & Natural Systems**
      1. Interactive cross-section of Earth's layers with temperature, pressure, and composition data
      2. Plate tectonics simulator showing continental drift over geological time
      3. Weather pattern lab where learners build pressure systems and predict storm outcomes
      4. Carbon cycle diagram with sliders for human activity and immediate climate feedback
      5. Local geology feature — identifies rock formations near the learner's location and connects them to global concepts

2. **Story 2 — History: Learn How Human Civilization Was Built**
   *As a learner seeking context, I want to journey through human history so that I can understand how societies rose, fell, and shaped the world we live in today.*

   1. **Topic 1 — Ancient Civilizations: Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece & Rome**
      1. Chronological world map that animates the rise and fall of empires between 3000 BCE and 500 CE
      2. Primary source reader with simplified translations and side-by-side commentary (Hammurabi's Code, Rosetta Stone)
      3. "Day in the Life" immersive narratives placing learners inside ancient households, markets, and temples
      4. Comparative feature showing parallel developments across civilizations at the same point in time
      5. Artifact gallery with 3D scans of real museum objects, each linked to a lesson

   2. **Topic 2 — Medieval World: Feudalism, Faith & Trade Routes**
      1. Feudal hierarchy pyramid learners can click through to explore roles, rights, and obligations
      2. Silk Road interactive map tracing goods, ideas, and diseases across continents 500–1500 CE
      3. Illustrated manuscript reader connecting illuminated texts to the cultural values of the era
      4. "Council Chamber" debate simulator where learners argue historical decisions (Crusades, Magna Carta)
      5. Audio narrations in period-appropriate regional accents for immersive storytelling

   3. **Topic 3 — Age of Exploration & Early Modernity (1400–1750)**
      1. Ship navigation challenge replicating dead-reckoning techniques used by explorers
      2. Colonial impact explorer showing Indigenous civilizations before and after contact
      3. Renaissance art timeline linking artistic innovations to scientific and philosophical shifts
      4. Trade economy simulation where learners manage supply, demand, and currency across empires
      5. Perspective toggle allowing learners to read the same event from multiple cultural viewpoints

   4. **Topic 4 — Revolutions & the Modern State (1750–1914)**
      1. Side-by-side comparison of American, French, and Haitian Revolutions — causes, events, outcomes
      2. Industrial Revolution factory sim showing how production, labor, and urbanization changed together
      3. Political philosophy primer linking Locke, Rousseau, and Marx to real revolutionary movements
      4. Newspaper headline archive with authentic front pages from key historical moments
      5. Legacy mapper showing how revolutionary ideals influenced later civil rights and independence movements

   5. **Topic 5 — 20th Century & Contemporary History**
      1. World War timeline with theater-by-theater breakdowns, casualty data, and turning points
      2. Cold War geopolitical map showing alliance shifts, proxy conflicts, and the nuclear arms race
      3. Decolonization wave tracker following independence movements continent by continent
      4. Oral history library with recorded testimonies from survivors of major 20th-century events
      5. "Then & Now" feature connecting a historical decision directly to a present-day consequence

3. **Story 3 — Language & Linguistics: Communicate Across Every Culture**
   *As a global citizen, I want to learn languages and understand how human communication works so that I can connect with more people and appreciate cultural diversity.*

   1. **Topic 1 — World Languages: Learning to Speak & Read**
      1. Spaced repetition vocabulary engine personalised to pace and retention curve per learner
      2. Conversational AI practice partner that conducts dialogues in the target language with error correction
      3. Pronunciation coach using device microphone with phoneme-level visual feedback
      4. Cultural context cards embedded in vocabulary lessons (e.g., formal vs. informal register in Japanese)
      5. Progress streaks and fluency milestones mapped to CEFR levels (A1 through C2)

   2. **Topic 2 — Grammar & Syntax: How Language Is Structured**
      1. Sentence diagram builder that visually breaks down subject, verb, object, and clause relationships
      2. Cross-language grammar comparator showing how tense, case, or gender works differently across languages
      3. Error correction game where learners spot and fix grammatically incorrect sentences
      4. Generative grammar explorer: learners modify rules and see which sentences become valid or invalid
      5. Adaptive grammar exercises that intensify focus on the specific patterns a learner repeatedly gets wrong

   3. **Topic 3 — Writing Systems: Scripts, Alphabets & Symbols**
      1. Stroke-order animation for logographic scripts (Chinese, Japanese kanji, Arabic, Devanagari)
      2. Historical evolution of the alphabet from Phoenician to Latin to modern Latin variants
      3. Handwriting practice canvas with real-time accuracy scoring against a reference model
      4. Cipher and code mini-game connecting ancient scripts (Linear B, cuneiform) to decipherment history
      5. Script comparison table showing how the same phoneme is represented across writing systems

   4. **Topic 4 — Linguistics: The Science of Language**
      1. Phonetics lab with interactive IPA chart, audio samples, and mouth-position diagrams
      2. Language family tree explorer mapping 7,000+ world languages back to proto-language roots
      3. Sociolinguistics case studies on dialects, code-switching, and language change over time
      4. Field linguistics challenge where learners analyze a fictional language corpus to extract grammar rules
      5. Endangered language spotlight series with community-recorded audio and cultural preservation context

   5. **Topic 5 — Language Acquisition: How We Learn to Communicate**
      1. Child language development timeline from babbling to full syntax with audio examples at each stage
      2. Bilingualism research explainer with interactive myth-vs-fact cards (e.g., "Does bilingualism cause confusion?")
      3. Immersion environment simulator that gradually increases the share of target-language content
      4. Neuroplasticity explainer showing how the brain reorganises during language learning at different ages
      5. Learning strategy selector that recommends methods (audio, visual, kinesthetic) based on learner self-assessment

4. **Story 4 — Mathematics: Master the Language of Patterns and Logic**
   *As an analytical thinker, I want to build mathematical understanding from first principles so that I can solve complex problems and see the patterns underlying science, art, and everyday life.*

   1. **Topic 1 — Arithmetic & Number Theory: The Foundation of Everything**
      1. Number line explorer from negative infinity to positive infinity with zoom-in to fractions and irrationals
      2. Prime factorisation visualiser using colour-coded factor trees
      3. Mental math challenge game that builds estimation and rounding intuition through speed rounds
      4. Historical journey through numeral systems (Roman, Babylonian, Mayan, Hindu-Arabic)
      5. Real-world word problem generator that personalises scenarios based on the learner's stated interests

   2. **Topic 2 — Geometry: Shape, Space & Proof**
      1. Dynamic geometry canvas (GeoGebra-style) where learners construct proofs by dragging points and lines
      2. 3D shape explorer with cross-section slicer to reveal interior geometry
      3. Proof-writing scaffold that breaks Euclidean proofs into labeled logical steps with hints
      4. Architecture and art connection gallery showing geometry in real buildings and famous paintings
      5. Non-Euclidean geometry introduction — spherical and hyperbolic surfaces with interactive comparisons

   3. **Topic 3 — Algebra: Variables, Equations & Functions**
      1. Equation balancer that visualises both sides of an equation as a scale, maintaining balance through operations
      2. Graphing engine that plots functions in real time as learners type or adjust parameters
      3. Algebra in the wild — lesson series connecting linear equations to phone plans, speed, and financial interest
      4. Factoring puzzle game using area models to build intuition before introducing formal methods
      5. Adaptive problem difficulty that adjusts based on response time and accuracy patterns

   4. **Topic 4 — Calculus: Change, Accumulation & Infinity**
      1. Limit intuition builder using animated sequences approaching a value from both sides
      2. Derivative visualiser showing the tangent line sliding along a curve as the learner scrubs through x values
      3. Integral area accumulator with Riemann sum animation converging to the definite integral
      4. Real-world calculus application modules: optimising a can's dimensions, modelling population growth
      5. Conceptual vs. computational toggle allowing learners to choose intuition-first or formula-first approaches

   5. **Topic 5 — Statistics & Probability: Reasoning Under Uncertainty**
      1. Live data explorer connecting public datasets (climate, sports, economics) to statistical concepts
      2. Probability simulator running thousands of trials instantly to build intuition for the law of large numbers
      3. Bias and fallacy detector — interactive case studies exposing misleading statistics in real news
      4. Bayesian reasoning walkthrough using medical test examples and visual probability trees
      5. Learner-designed experiment tool: define a hypothesis, collect peer data, and run statistical tests

5. **Story 5 — Arts, Philosophy & Culture: Explore What It Means to Be Human**
   *As a thoughtful individual, I want to engage with art, music, philosophy, and world cultures so that I can develop empathy, critical thinking, and a richer sense of what humanity has created and believed.*

   1. **Topic 1 — Visual Arts: Seeing, Making & Interpreting**
      1. Art movement timeline from Paleolithic cave painting through Modernism to contemporary digital art
      2. Guided "slow looking" sessions prompting learners to observe a single artwork for five structured minutes
      3. Technique workshop: learners follow step-by-step drawing and composition exercises with annotated examples
      4. Artist biography series connecting personal life, historical context, and recurring themes in the work
      5. Create & share studio where learners upload their own work for community feedback using guided critique frameworks

   2. **Topic 2 — Music: Sound, Structure & Culture**
      1. Interactive ear training module for identifying intervals, chords, and rhythmic patterns
      2. World music explorer with region maps linking musical traditions to cultural and historical context
      3. Composition sandbox with a loop-based sequencer and scale/mode selector for guided creation
      4. Music theory visualiser mapping scales, chords, and progressions on a colour-coded keyboard
      5. Listening journal feature where learners annotate recordings with timestamped observations and emotions

   3. **Topic 3 — Philosophy: Questions, Arguments & Ways of Thinking**
      1. Socratic dialogue simulator posing philosophical questions and responding to learner answers with follow-up challenges
      2. "Great Debates" module presenting opposing philosophical positions with structured pro/con analysis
      3. Thought experiment library (Trolley Problem, Ship of Theseus, Veil of Ignorance) with community poll data
      4. Logic and argumentation trainer identifying valid, invalid, and fallacious reasoning in real-world claims
      5. Philosophy of science strand connecting epistemology to how scientific knowledge is built and revised

   4. **Topic 4 — World Cultures: Traditions, Values & Diversity**
      1. Cultural atlas with interactive profiles of 200+ cultures covering food, ritual, language, and belief
      2. Festival calendar surfacing upcoming global celebrations with explainers on their origins and significance
      3. Storytelling tradition library with folklore and mythology audio narrated by native storytellers
      4. Cross-cultural comparison feature examining how different societies approach family, governance, and spirituality
      5. Respectful curiosity guide teaching learners how to engage with unfamiliar cultures without stereotyping

   5. **Topic 5 — Literature: Stories, Voice & the Written Word**
      1. Annotated reader with vocabulary help, historical context, and thematic commentary inline
      2. Genre explorer mapping the evolution of the novel, poetry, drama, and essay across world literatures
      3. Close reading workshops guiding learners through metre, metaphor, narrative structure, and voice
      4. Author in context series linking an author's biography, era, and social position to their literary choices
      5. Collaborative reading circles where small learner groups discuss a shared text using guided discussion prompts
